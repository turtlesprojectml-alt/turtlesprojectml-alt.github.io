Overview

At a glance

  • What 400 high-fidelity simulations of 3D turbulent wakes behind cylinders of varying shape, Reynolds number and angle of attack. Each has 400 time steps on an irregular mesh with 3–11 million points per frame.
  • Why it’s hard Real 3D turbulence with an active energy cascade, dense irregular meshes, and long time horizons, all in the same benchmark.
  • What we found Only one of four state-of-the-art neural operators stays stable over a 400-step rollout. Spectra expose lost or spurious small-scale energy, and the best surrogate is only about 16× faster than the solver.
Read the abstract

Machine-learning surrogates are increasingly used to accelerate computational fluid dynamics, yet progress is limited by the lack of benchmarks capturing realistic, time-dependent turbulent flows. We introduce TurtLES, a 14 TB dataset of 3D turbulent cylinder wakes in the shear-layer transition regime, computed via high-fidelity implicit large-eddy simulations. The dataset contains 400 trajectories of 400 time steps each, with 3–11 million points per frame, spanning variations in geometry, Reynolds number, and angle of attack. Unlike existing datasets, TurtLES combines (i) physically realistic turbulence with an active energy cascade driven by vortex stretching, (ii) dense irregular meshes, and (iii) long-horizon temporal dynamics. We benchmark state-of-the-art neural operators on autoregressive rollouts and identify key failure modes, including temporal instability, loss of small-scale energy, and challenges in learning on large irregular domains. TurtLES provides a new benchmark for evaluating surrogate models on 3D turbulent wakes at industrially relevant mesh resolution.

What existing benchmarks miss

Many “dynamic fluid” benchmarks are unsteady or chaotic without containing 3D turbulence, where vortex stretching drives energy from large eddies down to small ones. Others reach 3D and industrial scale but only at steady state, or stay on coarse point clouds. TurtLES covers all of these axes at once.

Dataset

400 turbulent wakes across a four-parameter design space

Each case places a super-elliptic cylinder in a uniform inflow. Four parameters define it: the shape family n, the half-length a, the Reynolds number Re, and the angle of attack α. Across this range the wake transitions to turbulence while the boundary layer stays laminar, so every trajectory contains an active 3D energy cascade.

Shape n
{1, 2, 5, ∞}: diamond, ellipse, rounded rectangle, rectangle (100 cases each)
Half-length a/D
1/6 to 3/2, log-uniform (aspect ratio 1/3 to 3); thickness fixed at D = 1
Reynolds number Re
1000 to 3900, based on D
Angle of attack α
0° to 45°

A Sobol’ sequence draws the 400 samples so that they cover the parameter space evenly, without clusters or gaps.

Simulation setup and validation

Each case is solved with a high-order Flux Reconstruction implicit LES scheme implemented in PyFR, at Mach 0.2 and Prandtl 0.71. The mesh is generated automatically with Gmsh: a 2D xy-plane with structured near-wall layers and an unstructured wake region, extruded into 30 structured span-wise layers over a span of 2πD. Each element holds 75–125 solution points, giving 2.9 M to 10.5 M unique points per case in the stored region.

The setup is validated against DNS and experimental data for four benchmark cases: a circular cylinder at Re = 1000 and 3900, a square cylinder at Re = 2000, and a 5:1 rectangle at Re = 3000. Strouhal number, drag, recirculation length, wake profiles and velocity spectra agree well with the literature; full comparisons and a mesh-convergence study are in the paper’s appendix.

What’s in a case

Each case stores one set of node coordinates and 400 snapshots of five fields: pressure p, density ρ, and velocity u, v, w. Snapshots start once the initial numerical transient has washed out, and cover the box x/D ∈ [−3, 10], y/D ∈ [−3.5, 3.5], z/D ∈ [0, 2π].

Storage format, sampling interval and probe spectra
  • Format. HDF5: one coordinate file plus four temporal chunks of 100 frames per trajectory.
  • Sampling interval. Δts = 0.04732 D/U, chosen to keep the inertial range of the turbulence spectrum. Sub-sampling acts in time only, so every snapshot keeps the full spatial resolution. For the circular cylinder, a trajectory spans about four vortex-shedding cycles.
  • Probe spectra. Each case also ships power spectra of u and v at three centreline probes 2.5D, 3.5D and 4.5D behind the object, with the script used to compute them. They give per-case evidence of the turbulent cascade and a physical target for evaluating surrogates.

Wakes across shapes and Reynolds numbers

At low Re the wake is made of fewer, thicker and more coherent vortex tubes. At high Re it breaks up into a much denser field of fine-scale structures.

Benchmark

Where today’s neural operators break

We train four neural operators, UPT, GINO, Transolver++ and CALM-PDE, to predict the next frame of all five fields, then roll each one out autoregressively for 399 steps from the first frame alone. All four share the same training budget: 320 training trajectories, 100k steps, about 20 hours on one H100.

1 of 4

stays stable

Over 400 steps, only CALM-PDE keeps its error below the mean-field level. The others drift to 5–33× that level.

2 of 4

no better than the mean

Even on the first frame, GINO and Transolver++ do no better than predicting each frame’s spatial average.

lost or invented

Energy spectra show UPT losing fine-scale fidelity and Transolver++ adding spurious small-scale energy.

faster than the solver

At millions of points per frame, the speedup over the solver is modest, from about 3× to 16×.

Training and evaluation protocol
  • Task. Predict the residual between frames t and t+1 from a single input frame, conditioned on Re, with an MSE loss on the residual. Shape and angle are not given to the model.
  • Training. 320 training trajectories, 524,288 sampled nodes per step, 100k steps at batch size 2, AdamW (lr 10−4, weight decay 10−3), 5k warm-up steps then cosine decay. No baseline received extra tuning.
  • Evaluation. 40 test trajectories, each rolled out for 399 steps on the full mesh. Metrics are relative L2 error, variance-scaled RMSE (VRMSE = 1 matches predicting the per-frame spatial mean), RMS log-power error of span-wise u spectra in low, mid and high wavenumber bands, and GPU time per trajectory.

Get the data

Splits and smaller subsets

Trajectories come pre-split into 320 training, 40 validation and 40 test cases. Every shape family is split exactly 80/10/10, and the assignment is optimised so that each subset covers the same range of Re, α and a as the full dataset. A circular-cylinder reference case (Re = 1000, α = 0°) is distributed separately.

Pick a mini-TurtLES subset

The full dataset is 14 TB. mini-TurtLES defines fixed, named subsets so results from different labs stay comparable: drop shape families, keep fewer snapshots, or both.

Scope

Limitations

  • One physical regime: turbulent cylinder wakes in the shear-layer transition range. Whether the failure modes transfer to other geometries or Reynolds numbers is open.
  • The reference is implicit LES, so surrogate errors are measured against this solver rather than DNS or experiment.
  • Snapshots are sub-sampled in time. The trajectory window is too short for converged averaged statistics such as Reynolds stresses.
  • The four baselines are a starting point, not an exhaustive survey of neural operators on this class of flows.
